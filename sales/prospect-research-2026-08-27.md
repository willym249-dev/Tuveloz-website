## What "qualified" means here

1. Publicly runs Instagram creator, ambassador, UGC, or gifting campaigns —
   their own site says so, not a directory.
2. Small enough that deliverable QA is plausibly manual, so a $99 flat pilot is
   a no-meeting decision for whoever reads the DM.
3. Reachable by a public route: Instagram DM to the handle their official site
   lists, or their site contact form. No harvested emails.
4. US-directed campaigns preferred, since receipts flag FTC-style disclosure
   gaps among the other brief items.

## FTC references used for education content

- [Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
  (also as [PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508_1.pdf)):
  disclosure must be hard to miss and placed with the endorsement; disclosures
  "are likely to be missed if they appear only on an ABOUT ME or profile page,
  at the end of posts or videos, or anywhere that requires a person to click
  MORE"; "ad," "advertisement," "sponsored," and thanking the brand for a free
  product are acceptable forms; on picture platforms like Instagram Stories,
  superimpose the disclosure over the picture with enough time to read it; in
  video, the disclosure belongs in the video itself, not only the description.
- [FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking).
