## Post 1 — the #ad that technically existed

**Visual:** plain receipt-style text card, monospaced, off-white background:

```
BRIEF SAYS   #ad in the first line
POST SHOWS   #ad — hashtag 24 of 28,
             behind "… more"
STATUS       MISS
```

**Caption (draft):**

The brief said: “#ad in the first line.”

The finished post had #ad. Technically. It was hashtag 24 of 28, three taps
behind “more,” below the fold where nobody goes.

Nobody lied here. The creator did include the tag. The brand did write the
requirement. It just didn’t survive the trip from the brief to the caption box
— and nobody’s job was to notice.

The FTC’s guide for influencers (“Disclosures 101”) is blunt about this exact
case: disclosures are likely to be missed when they sit at the end of a post
or anywhere that makes people click MORE. Placement is the rule, not the tag.

This example is a composite we built to show the pattern — it isn’t a
client’s post. But if you run creator campaigns, you have captions like this
right now, and finding them takes one boring pass nobody makes time for.

That pass is the whole product. One brief, up to 10 finished posts, one plain
receipt per post: BRIEF SAYS → POST SHOWS → MATCH or MISS. Founding pilot is
$99 flat. Not legal advice — it’s a careful reading of your own brief against
your own posts, and every decision after that is yours.

## Post 2 — the usage window nobody put on a calendar

**Visual:** same receipt-style card:

```
BRIEF SAYS   usage: 90 days from live date
POST LIVE    Mar 3
LAST DAY     Jun 1
BOOSTED      Jul 14  ← day 133
STATUS       MISS
```

**Caption (draft):**

There’s a line in most creator briefs that reads like fine print and behaves
like a deadline: “Usage: 90 days from live date.”

It’s a calendar entry. Almost nobody makes the calendar entry.

So: post goes live March 3. The brand boosts it in April — inside the window,
fine. Someone re-boosts the same post in July, because it performed. That’s
day 133 of a 90-day license. The creator granted exactly what the brief said
and nothing more, and now the brand is running paid media on content it no
longer has rights to use. Not out of malice — out of nobody checking.

This one is a composite we built to show the pattern, not a client’s
campaign. The fix is embarrassingly small: write down the live date and the
window, per post, somewhere someone will look.

That’s literally a receipt line. One brief, up to 10 finished posts, one
plain receipt per post — live date, window, last day, and whether what
happened matches what the brief said. Founding pilot is $99 flat. Not legal
advice — it’s your own brief, read carefully against your own posts. What you
do with each line is your call.

**Alt text (draft):** Receipt-style card showing a 90-day usage window from a
March 3 live date, last day June 1, and a boost on July 14 — day 133 —
marked MISS.
