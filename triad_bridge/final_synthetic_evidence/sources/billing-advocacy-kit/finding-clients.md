# Finding the first clients

Ordered by how fast they produce a real case, not by how big the channel is. You need **three completed cases** before anything else matters — they are what let you find out whether the process actually runs.

---

## The rules that keep this clean

Written first because the fastest ways to get clients here are also the ways to become the thing people warn each other about.

1. **Never target anyone by medical condition or diagnosis.** Not by search, not by list, not by inference. It is the line between advocacy and something much uglier.
2. **Never cold-contact someone because you learned they have a bill.** They come to you.
3. **Never sell fear, and never quote a figure you have not sourced.** "You could be sued" closes deals and builds a reputation you cannot undo. So does a statistic you cannot back. The pitch is that a bill nobody has read line by line is worth reading — calm, defensible, and enough.
4. **Never break a community's self-promotion rules.** One ban costs you the channel permanently.
5. **Always mention the free options.** Every time, unprompted. It is the whole trust position and it costs you very little in practice.

---

## 1. Your own network — the first case comes from here

Not a growth channel. The way you get case number one this month.

Everyone knows someone with a hospital bill they think is wrong. Say plainly what you are doing and that you will do the first few free to build the operations rehearsal. Ask people to think of one person, not to spread the word.

**Who not to take:** anyone billed by anywhere in the health system you work for — affiliates and employed physician groups included. That is in the client agreement and it is not negotiable.

Do the first two or three at no charge deliberately. You are buying a rehearsal of your own process and, if they agree, a person who will say you were straight with them.

## 2. Referral partners who see the problem and have nothing to offer

The strongest channel, and an underused one. You are looking for people who meet Marylanders with unaffordable bills **and currently have no answer for them**.

| Who | Why they refer | What to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bankruptcy attorneys** | They turn away people whose entire problem is one $12,000 hospital bill. That is not a bankruptcy, and they have nowhere to send them | "If someone comes to you and their only debt is a hospital bill, I'll look at it. No charge at all — I'm not taking payment from anyone while I get this right." |
| **Community health centers and free clinics** | See uninsured patients constantly, know the bills are coming, cannot help with them | Bring the Maryland facts, not a pitch — most staff do not know assistance is statutory at 200% FPL |
| **Church benevolence funds** | Get asked for money toward medical bills every month, and have no way to check whether the bill is even right | "Before the fund pays it, let me check whether it is correct or whether they qualify for free care." That saves the fund money |
| **Social workers and discharge planners** | Watch the bill problem start | Same as the clinics |
| **Credit counselors and financial coaches** | Medical debt lands on their desk with no path to fix it | They already refer out — get on the list |

**How to approach:** one page, not a brochure. What you do, that it costs the person nothing at all, and the free options you always name first. Ask for nothing on the first contact except permission to leave the page.

The church and benevolence-fund angle deserves particular attention. **You save the fund money** — that is a genuinely different conversation from asking them for something.

## 3. The 240-day window is a targeting mechanism

This is the Maryland-specific one, and it is the sharpest idea here.

Maryland lets income be re-tested for a change in circumstances **within 240 days after the first bill**. So anyone who lost a job, lost hours, or got sick in roughly the last eight months — and has a hospital bill from *before* that happened — may now qualify for free or reduced care they did not qualify for at the time.

That is a real, findable population, and nobody is telling them:

- Workforce development and unemployment offices
- Union locals after a layoff
- Anywhere a layoff was publicly announced
- Community organisations serving people in transition

The message writes itself and it is entirely true: **"If your income dropped after your hospital bill arrived, Maryland may still cover it. You have 240 days."**

That is a public service announcement that happens to describe your business. Lead with it.

## 4. Places where people already post bills asking for help

People post photographs of their bills asking "is this right?" every day. Answering well, in public, for free, is the cheapest reputation available.

Where: the large personal finance and health insurance communities, plus Maryland-local and county-level groups on Reddit, Facebook, and Nextdoor.

**How to do it without getting banned:**

- **Answer the question completely and expect nothing.** Point to what is worth *asking about*, name the specific thing to request, and mention the free options. **Never tell a stranger in public that a charge is wrong** — you have not seen the record, and a confident public diagnosis is how this goes badly.
- **Never pitch in a reply. Never DM someone who posted.** Both get you banned, and both make you the thing people screenshot.
- Let your profile say what you do. People who need more will ask.
- Read each community's self-promotion rules before the first post, not after a removal.

This is slow and it compounds. Ten genuinely useful public answers do more than any ad, because the person reading has watched you be right about someone else's bill.

## 5. Search, once the page is up

"Maryland hospital bill help", "hospital charity care Maryland", "medical bill too high Maryland", "denied claim appeal Maryland" are real searches by people in trouble right now. The site is the seed. This is a months-long channel, not a launch channel — start it now precisely because it is slow.

---

## What to say when someone asks what you charge

> "Nothing — it's free while I'm getting started. First thing I check is whether you qualify for financial assistance, because Maryland sets that by law, and if you do there are non-profits who'll help you claim it free and I'll point you at them. What I do is go through the bill line by line and write up everything worth asking about, with the source for each point, and you send it yourself. It's your bill and your name on it."

That answers price, risk, and "why not just use the free ones" in one breath, and it has the advantage of being simple because there is no fee to explain.

## The measure for the first ninety days

**Three completed cases is an operations rehearsal, not a operations rehearsal.** It tells you whether the process runs. It tells you nothing reliable about outcomes, and it must never be described to anyone as evidence that the service works.

What to record from the first cases, because you cannot reason about any of it later without the data:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Funnel | Enquiries, screened out and why, engaged, completed |
| Labour | Your hours per case, split between review, drafting, and follow-up |
| Hold and contact | Time on hold, calls needed, letters sent, replies received |
| Deadlines | Every one identified, met, or missed |
| Cost | Every real cost per case, including your time at a real rate |
| Outcome | What changed, what did not, what was refused and why |
| Reversals | Anything corrected then reinstated, or a question that turned out wrong |
| Incidents | Every privacy or handling near-miss, however small |

The last two matter most and are the ones people stop recording first. A question you raised that turned out to be wrong is the single most useful thing in the file.
