# The first call

What you ask, in order, the first time someone contacts you. Twelve minutes. You are deciding three things: whether there is anything here worth chasing, whether they qualify for assistance, and whether you can take them at all.

**Take notes in the questionnaire below as you go.** Do not promise an outcome on this call. Do not quote a fee before you know whether assistance applies.

---

## Open

> "Tell me what happened, and what they're asking you to pay."

Then stop talking. People need to tell the story before they can answer anything, and the story usually contains the case. Do not rush them to the numbers.

**Two things worth saying early, because most people are carrying them.** That a bill like this does not mean they did anything wrong. And that they do not need to understand any of it to be helped — that is your job, not theirs. Neither costs you anything and both change how the rest of the call goes.

## The four screening questions

**1. "Is this bill from anywhere in [the health system you work for]?"**
Ask it first — and it means the **whole system**: affiliates, subsidiaries, joint ventures, employed physician groups, anything billing under its name or tax ID. If yes, you stop — say plainly that you work there, that it would be a conflict, and refer them out. This one is not negotiable and it is better to lose the call in the first minute than the tenth.

**2. "Roughly how many people are in your household, and roughly what does the household bring in?"**
Not exact numbers, and say so — people brace for a means test. Run `assistance-screen.mjs` while you are on the call.

**Maryland's mandated floor** (a hospital may be more generous, never less):

| Household income | The hospital must give at least |
| --- | --- |
| ≤ 200% of poverty | **Free care** |
| 201–250% | **75% off** |
| 251–300% | **60% off** |
| Below 500% with hardship | Reduced cost, where 12-month family medical debt tops 25% of income |

For a household of four in 2026 that is roughly: free to about $66,000; 75% off to about $82,500; 60% off to about $99,000.

**This applies regardless of citizenship or immigration status.** Say so plainly if it might matter — many people assume it does not and never apply. If they hesitate, say why you are asking: *"Maryland requires hospitals to give free care under a certain income and most people are never told."*

**3. "Has anything changed with your income since the bill arrived?"**
The 240-day question. Job lost, hours cut, out sick. It reopens cases that look closed and nobody else asks it.

**4. "Has this gone to collections, and have you had any letter about a lawsuit or a court date?"**
A court date changes everything and is the one thing that makes this urgent. If there is a lawsuit, they need a lawyer, not you — say so.

## Then the case questions

- "Do you have an itemized bill, or just a summary?" (Almost always a summary. Explain the difference — it is often the moment they realise nobody has ever actually looked.)
- "Did insurance process this, or were you self-pay?"
- "Do you have the explanation of benefits from your insurer?"
- "How many separate bills came out of this one visit?" (Prompt: hospital, radiologist, anesthesiologist, lab, ambulance.)
- "Were you given a written estimate beforehand?" (Self-pay only — this is the good-faith-estimate route.)
- "Was there a facility fee, and were you told about it when you booked?"
- "Have you already applied for financial assistance, or been told about it?"

## What you say about money

> "Nothing. This is free while I'm getting started — no fee, no percentage, no card, no deposit. If that ever changes you'll be told in writing first and you can walk away.
>
> First thing I do is check whether you qualify for financial assistance, because in Maryland that's set by law rather than by the hospital. If you qualify, that's yours by right and there are non-profits — Dollar For — who help with those applications free. The Attorney General's office mediates billing disputes free too, and I'll tell you about both.
>
> What I do is go through the bill line by line and write up everything worth asking about, with the source for each point. **You** read it, sign it, and send it. It's your bill and your name — I don't speak for you."

## How you close

> "I can't take your documents yet — there isn't a secure way to receive them, and I'm not going to have you email a medical bill. When that's ready I'll tell you exactly what to send and why."

Then: send the pilot terms so they can read them in their own time. **No document list and no secure link until a tested portal exists.** Open the file the same day and diary any deadline **before** you do anything else.

## When to route them elsewhere

Say it on the call rather than going quiet afterwards. Any of these goes to qualified help, not into your file:

- **Their facility is your employer.**
- **A lawsuit, judgment, garnishment, lien, or court date** — they need a lawyer, today.
- **Bankruptcy, an estate, workers' compensation, or an injury lien** on the matter.
- **A deadline inside seven days** — too tight to prepare properly; send them straight to HEAU.
- **A complex medical-necessity denial** — that needs clinical review, not a bill audit.
- **A minor, or someone acting under guardianship or power of attorney.**
- **Psychiatric or substance-use treatment records** — these carry extra protection and extra rules.
- The bill is small enough that the effort will not pay either of you. Do not just decline — tell them exactly what to ask for themselves, in words they can use, and then let them go. A five-minute answer someone can act on is worth more to them than a file.
- They want you to negotiate a balance they agree is correct. That is debt settlement and it is out of scope. Explain the difference in one sentence: *"If the charge is right, it's right — what I do is find out whether it's right."*
