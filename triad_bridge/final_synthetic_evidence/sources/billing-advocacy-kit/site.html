Before anything else, we check whether you should be paying at all

Maryland law sets a floor that hospitals cannot go below. Free medically necessary care at or below 200% of the federal poverty level. Above that, a required sliding scale: at least 75% off between 201% and 250%, and at least 60% off between 251% and 300%. Below 500% there is a separate hardship route if your family's medical debt over twelve months is more than a quarter of your income.

A hospital may be more generous than that. It may not be less. And this applies to qualified Maryland residents regardless of citizenship or immigration status.

If you are somewhere in the middle — too far above the line for free care, nowhere near able to pay — that sliding scale is the part worth asking about by name.

If you qualify, we tell you — and we charge you nothing on anything written off that way. We will also point you to Dollar For, a non-profit that helps with those applications for free, and to the Maryland Attorney General's Health Education and Advocacy Unit, which mediates billing disputes at no cost.

We would much rather tell you that on the first call than have you pay for something you could have had free. If one of those services is the whole answer for you, that is a good outcome and we will say so.

What Maryland already gives you

  • Free care under 200% of the poverty level

    Not the hospital's choice — state law. Reduced cost below 500% with hardship.

  • 240 days to re-test your income

    If you lost hours or lost a job after the bill arrived, your situation now can count. It is worth asking about.

  • Medical debt stays off your credit report

    Since October 2025, providers, their agents, and collectors cannot report it.

  • No interest if you qualify for assistance

    And no lien on your home over a medical debt.

  • Hospital rates are regulated here

    Maryland is the only state where a commission sets hospital rates. Your charges sit inside a structure that can be asked about.

How it works

  1. Intake is not open

    Please do not send us any records. There is no secure way to receive them yet. When there is, we will ask for one document at a time and tell you exactly why each is needed.

  2. We check assistance first

    Household size and rough income. If you qualify for free or reduced care, that comes before anything else.

  3. You request the itemized bill

    You may never have been sent one — it is not what hospitals send by default. We tell you exactly what to ask for and what words to use, and you make the request.

  4. We read every line

    Against the codes, the dates, what your insurance allowed, and the hospital's own published prices.

  5. We write it up for you to send

    A packet for the hospital, and for each separate company that billed you. You read it, sign it, and send it — with a script for the call if you want one.

  6. You get a written report

    What we questioned, what was asked, and what changed. Yours to keep.

What we are not

  • We are not lawyers, and nothing here is legal advice. We do not represent you to anyone — you send your own letters and make your own calls, and we help you prepare them.
  • We are not doctors, and we do not review whether care was appropriate.
  • We do not negotiate or settle debts. If a charge is correct, it is correct — our work is establishing what you actually owe.
  • We do not touch homeowners, auto, or property claims.
  • We cannot promise a result, and we cannot tell you from a bill alone whether a charge was medically correct — some questions need your chart and a qualified coder. We tell you which is which.
