Maryland · Pilot · Scripts for the patient

Billing Desk Playbook

The calls, in order, once per account. Tap any script to copy it.

During the pilot the patient makes every call, in their own name. These are their scripts. You coach, you prepare, you listen in if they want — you do not speak for them. Never balance negotiation.

STOP

A HIPAA authorization is not authority to represent anyone. It lets a provider release records — it does not let you appeal, negotiate, settle, or speak as someone's representative. During the pilot the patient calls about their own account, which needs no authorization at all. Have the account number and dates of service in front of them before they dial.

Log every call — all four, no exceptions

  • Date & time
  • Who you spoke to
  • What they committed to
  • Date you'll chase it

Half of what you win comes from being the only party who wrote down what was promised.

CALL 2

Financial assistance, before anything else

Always second, even when you're sure they earn too much. Published eligibility limits vary enormously between hospitals — the same family qualifies at one and not at another across town.

Then this exact question — the answer decides your whole fee

The answer is usually separately. That's Call 5, and it's where a lot of the real money sits.

Maryland — free care is the law, not the hospital's choice

  1. Hospitals must provide free medically necessary care at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, and reduced-cost care below 500% where there's financial hardship. This is statute, not policy. Say so.
  2. Income counts as of the time of service or as changed within 240 days after the first bill. Ask every client, every time, the question nobody asks — and it's below.
  3. Since 1 October 2025: medical debt can't be reported to credit bureaus by the provider, its agents, or collectors; no lien on a primary residence over it; and no interest may be charged to anyone who qualifies for free or reduced-cost care. Say all three to a frightened client on the first call.
  4. If the account is with a collection agency, the provider–agency contract must carry the credit-reporting prohibition. Without it that contract is void and unenforceable — worth asking about.

Ask this of every Maryland client — it re-opens cases that look closed

If they qualify: tell the client, help them apply, and charge nothing on the write-off. Say plainly that Dollar For does this free and give them the choice. You are not charging anyone anyway, and you gain someone you were straight with.

CALL 4

Insurance, when the denial is the problem

Different building, different rules. The patient calls about their own claim. Nobody acts for the patient during the pilot, so no such authority is needed or held.

Three questions that decide whether you appeal or just fix a mistake

  1. "Was this denied for medical necessity, or is this a coding or eligibility issue?" — coding and eligibility problems get reprocessed, not appealed. Far faster.
  2. "Can this be sent back for reprocessing rather than appeal?"
  3. "What's the exact deadline, and where do I send it?"

Work out the appeal deadline on day one, and record how it was determined. Do not assume it runs from the date on the letter — depending on the plan it may run from notice or from receipt, and the controlling document is the denial notice or the summary plan description, not a rule of thumb. Have a person verify it against that document. A missed deadline is serious, but it is not automatically unrecoverable — that depends on the plan and the reason. Never tell a consumer either that it can be fixed or that it cannot.

Escalation ladder

Move up one rung per unanswered promise. Don't skip rungs; don't sit on rung one forever.

  1. Billing representative
  2. Billing supervisor — "I've called three times, spoken to [names], and been told twice this was under review. Can I speak with a supervisor?"
  3. Patient advocate or patient relations office — most hospitals have one, few patients ask
  4. The facility's compliance line, on coding accuracy specifically
  5. Maryland only — hospital rates here are set by the Health Services Cost Review Commission, the only such regulator in the country. Ask it as a question, never a threat: "Can you show me how this charge reconciles to the hospital's HSCRC-approved rate structure?" A Maryland billing office knows exactly what that means.
  6. Maryland — HEAU. The Attorney General's Health Education and Advocacy Unit mediates medical billing and insurance disputes free, including financial assistance applications and debt collection. Tell every client it exists, unprompted — it is free and for many people it is the whole answer, not a fallback. Where a consumer chooses to go to HEAU, a complete file helps them present their own case. We have no evidence that anything we prepare changes an HEAU outcome, and no such claim may be made.
  7. Written complaint to the Maryland Insurance Administration on the plan side
  8. For non-profit hospitals: charity care obligations are a condition of their tax status, and saying so out loud on rung four changes the tone

What works, what ends the call

Works

  • Account number in the first sentence
  • Naming a specific line and a specific code
  • Asking who owns the next step, and by what date
  • Repeating back what they said
  • Everything in writing the same day
  • Being unfailingly pleasant to someone who didn't cause this

Ends badly

  • Anger at the representative
  • Vague "this is too high"
  • Threatening to sue
  • Guessing at codes, or asserting a charge is wrong when it is a question to ask
  • Claiming to speak for someone you have no authority to speak for

The wall — absolute

Never touch your employer's systems for any of this. Not to look up an account, not to check a policy, not to "just see" something. Knowledge of how hospitals work is yours and walks out the door with you. Access to a specific patient record does not, and using it is a firing and potentially a criminal matter.

Never take a client billed by anywhere in the health system you work for — affiliates, subsidiaries, joint ventures, and employed physician groups included. No coworkers, and no lead that came through work. If one turns up at intake, say so immediately and refer it out. It's in the client agreement for exactly this reason.
