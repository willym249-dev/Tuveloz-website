You meet people whose only real problem is one hospital bill they cannot pay. That is not a bankruptcy and not a benevolence case — it is a bill nobody has read line by line, sitting with someone who is frightened of it. Send them to me and it costs them nothing — this is a free pilot and I am not charging anyone.

What I do

  • Show them how to get the itemized bill — a summary is what hospitals send by default, and a summary cannot be reviewed
  • Read every line against the codes, the dates, the quantities, and what their insurance actually allowed
  • Write up everything worth questioning, with a source cited for each point, for them to sign and send
  • Prepare their hospital financial assistance application, which they submit
  • Prepare an insurance appeal for them to file, with the deadline worked out
  • Cover every separate bill from the same visit — radiology, anesthesia, pathology, lab. One visit can produce four to six, and they are easy to miss

What it costs the person you send

Nothing. No fee, no percentage, no deposit, no card. This is running as a free pilot while I build the first cases and while I put a written registration question to the Maryland Office of Financial Regulation. Nobody is charged anything until that comes back, and not then without being told in writing first.

I never take custody of anyone's money, and I never take a share of financial assistance — that is theirs by law.

They stay in charge of their own bill. I prepare the packet; they read it, sign it, and send it. I do not speak for anyone.

The free services, which I mention every time

You should know I tell every client about these before I do anything, and you should feel free to send people straight to them instead of to me.

  • Dollar For — a non-profit that helps with hospital charity care applications at no cost
  • The Maryland Attorney General's Health Education and Advocacy Unit — free mediation of medical billing and insurance disputes, with its own published outcome figures
  • SHIP — free Medicare counselling

What I add is the part those services do not have capacity for: going through the bill line by line and writing up every point worth raising, with its source, so that what gets sent is coherent.

Two things worth knowing even if you never send anyone

The 240-day window. Maryland lets a hospital re-test income for a change in circumstances up to 240 days after the first bill. So someone who lost a job or lost hours after the bill arrived may qualify for free care now even though they did not at the time. It is worth asking about. If you take one thing from this page, take that.

Since 1 October 2025. Medical debt may not be reported to credit bureaus by providers, their agents, or collectors. No money judgment may lien a primary residence over it. And no interest may be charged to anyone who qualifies for free or reduced-cost care.
