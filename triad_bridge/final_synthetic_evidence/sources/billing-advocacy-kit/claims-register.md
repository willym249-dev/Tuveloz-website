## CORRECTION — the "it's just arithmetic" argument was overstated

An earlier version of this register said registration costs "$1,000 a year plus the premium on a $50,000 bond — call it $1,500–3,000 annually," and concluded that excluding balance negotiation was **arithmetic rather than caution**.

**That rested on a contradiction inside this very document.** One source states the $50,000 bond applies where the provider "establishes a bank account for holding its customer's money." Another presents it as a flat requirement of registration. This kit's own service model **never holds client money**, so if the first reading is correct the bond may not apply at all and registration may cost roughly **$1,000 a year, not $1,500–3,000**.

So:

- **No registration cost figure in this kit is usable.** Neither "$1,000 plus a $50,000 bond" nor the externally asserted "$400 plus $400, bond conditional" has been read from enacted text. State no figure at all until § 12-1006 and § 12-1014 are read directly.
- **The arithmetic argument is withdrawn** as the headline reason for excluding balance negotiation. The remaining reasons stand on their own and are stronger: the classification is unresolved, § 12-1010 bars collecting any fee until the consumer has made a payment, and balance negotiation is the one activity squarely inside the statutory definition.
- **Verify the enacted text of the bond provision** before any registration decision.

This is exactly the failure this register exists to catch, and it survived two passes before an outside review found it.

## Analysis: the scope question, worked from the actual statutory text

The definition was retrieved. Working it clause by clause, because the wording decides more than the summary did.

> "any service or program **represented, directly or by implication,** to renegotiate, settle, reduce, or **in any way alter the terms** of payment or other terms of a debt between a consumer and one or more unsecured creditors or debt collectors, **including a reduction in the balance**, interest rate, or fees owed"

**Two clauses do the work, and neither was visible in the summary.**

**"Represented, directly or by implication."** The trigger attaches to what you *hold yourself out as doing*, not only to what you actually do. **Your marketing language is part of the legal test.** A site saying "we cut your medical bills" has represented a balance reduction and is arguably inside the Act before a single call is made. A site saying "we find billing errors and establish what you actually owe" represents something different. This is free to get right and expensive to get wrong, and it is the single most actionable thing in this document.

**"In any way alter... including a reduction in the balance."** Deliberately broad, and it names balance reduction explicitly.

### Where each service lands

| Service | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| **Insurance appeals** | **Unresolved — strongest argument of the three.** A health plan deciding coverage is not an "unsecured creditor or debt collector," and an appeal is not an alteration of a debt's terms. That reading is strong but it is still an argument, not a determination. |
| **Billing error correction** | **Unresolved — strong argument for outside.** You are not renegotiating a debt down from $Y — you are establishing that $Y was never the correct amount. That is a challenge to validity, not an alteration of terms. **The risk:** the *effect* is a smaller balance, and a regulator may read outcomes rather than theories. |
| **Financial assistance advocacy** | **Unresolved, and the weakest of the three.** The argument for outside is that you apply for a statutory hospital program rather than negotiating with a creditor. But the outcome is a reduced or eliminated balance, and "in any way alter" is broad. Do not treat this one as settled. |
| **Balance negotiation** | **Clearly inside.** Correctly excluded from scope. |

### Two corrections, in order

**First:** an earlier version said all three non-negotiation services were "arguably outside" with equal confidence. That was too even — appeals are much safer than assistance advocacy.

**Second, and more important:** a later version then called appeals "clearly outside." That overcorrected. **None of the three is resolved.** The table above gives the strength of each argument, and an argument is not a classification. Until written guidance arrives from OFR or Maryland counsel, no document in this kit may state that any service falls outside the Act.

### Get this answered without a lawyer

The right body is not a law firm — it is the **Maryland Office of the Commissioner of Financial Regulation**, which administers this Act and is who would enforce it. OFR publishes industry advisories and responds to licensing-scope enquiries, and it has a demonstrated record of issuing, revising, and rescinding formal guidance as the law changes.

Ask them, in writing, one narrow question: *does a service that reviews medical bills for errors, disputes incorrect charges, assists with hospital financial assistance applications, and represents patients in health insurance appeals — and that does not negotiate balances with creditors and does not handle consumer funds — require registration under Fin. Inst. Title 12, Subtitle 10?*

A written answer from the regulator is worth more than an opinion letter, and it costs nothing. **Keep whatever they send.** If the answer is "yes, register," that is still a good outcome to have early rather than after taking clients.
