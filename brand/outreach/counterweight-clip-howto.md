# How to shoot the counterweight clip

Plain-language companion to
[founder-counterweight-clip.md](founder-counterweight-clip.md), which holds the
creative rules. This file is the doing part: what to buy, what to press, what
order to do it in.

**Picture and voice are recorded separately.** You film your hands saying
nothing, then record the line afterwards sitting in your car. I lay the voice
over the picture and the real driveway sound stays underneath. Nobody can tell,
and it removes the one thing that can waste the whole trip — wind roaring over
your voice in a way you won't notice until you're back inside.

## You are making four files

| # | File | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hand-action take — silent | 12–15s |
| 2 | Hand-action take again — silent | 12–15s |
| 3 | Driveway ambience — nobody talking | 10–15s |
| 4 | Voice memo of the line, inside the parked car | ~12s |

---

## The one thing people get wrong

Your hands are the subject, so **you cannot hold the phone.** Something else
has to hold it or the shot does not exist. That is the entire reason the
equipment list below isn't just "a phone."

---

## Equipment

### You already have

| Thing | Notes |
|---|---|
| Smartphone | Any phone from the last ~5 years |
| A car | Yours, or one you have permission to film |
| Real tools | Sockets, a rag, a ratchet, a dipstick |
| A rag and a tray or toolbox | Gives your hands something honest to do |

### Worth buying — about $20

**A small phone tripod with a clamp.** The only thing actually worth spending
money on. Propping the phone on a fender works right up until it slides off
mid-take, and a tripod holds chest height exactly where this shot lives — plus
it lets you shoot take 2 from the identical position.

### You do not need

Lights, gimbal, ring light, microphone, editing app, external recorder. A mic
is unnecessary because the voice is recorded separately in a quiet car. Every
other item makes the post look more produced, which is the opposite of the job.

## Before you leave the house

1. **Clean the camera lens** on your shirt. Highest-value ten seconds of the
   whole shoot.
2. **Check 5 GB free.** 4K video is large.
3. **Charge past 50%.**
4. **Turn on Do Not Disturb.** A notification chime lands in the audio and
   there is no removing it.
5. **Set the camera to vertical video, 4K/30** — or 1080p/30 if 4K isn't
   offered. Not 60fps, not 24fps, not slow motion, not any "cinematic" mode.
6. **Never rotate the phone sideways.** Vertical the whole time.

## Setting up outside

1. Pick your spot — real driveway or lot in Montgomery County. Late afternoon
   light is kinder than noon, but don't wait days for perfect light.
2. Open the hood. **Engine off and cool, parking brake on, keys out of the
   ignition.** Nothing here needs a running or warm engine.
3. Phone on the tripod at **chest height, 3–4 feet** from where your hands will
   be. Close enough that hands and tools fill much of the frame, far enough
   that you can move without leaving it.
4. **Aim so your face is out of frame** — chest down. Check it on screen now;
   far easier than cropping later.
5. Sweep the frame for things that must not be in it:
   - Licence plate — move the phone or the car
   - House number, neighbour's door, anyone else's property
   - **Tool brand names.** Snap-on, DeWalt and Milwaukee all put their logo
     exactly where your hands go. Turn the tool so the logo faces away.
   - Any logo on your own clothing
   - Bystanders
6. **Tap and hold on your hands on screen** until it says AE/AF Lock. Stops the
   picture hunting focus mid-take.
7. Find the wind and **stand so it hits your back.**

## Files 1 and 2 — the hand-action takes

1. Hit record.
2. Wait one full second doing nothing. Count it: *one thousand one.*
3. Do something real with your hands for 12–15 seconds. **Do not mime** — mimed
   hands look fake, and this whole post exists to not look fake.

   Safe, honest actions: sorting sockets into a tray, wiping a tool down with a
   rag, pulling and reading the dipstick, laying a ratchet on the fender,
   closing a toolbox latch.

   **Do not turn any fastener.** Nothing loosened, nothing tightened, no repair
   work on a car you don't know. Beyond the safety reason, it casts you as a
   mechanic — and you're the founder building Tuveloz, which is both truer and
   the whole reason the line works.
4. **Say nothing.** The voice comes later.
5. Stop recording.
6. **Do it again.** The second take is usually better, because by then you've
   stopped thinking about the camera.

## File 3 — the ambience

1. Leave the phone exactly where it is.
2. Hit record and step back out of the way.
3. Stand completely still for 15 seconds. No talking, no footsteps, no touching
   the phone, engine off.
4. If a siren, leaf blower, barking dog or neighbour's conversation lands in the
   middle, **record it again.** Clean means clean.

This file matters more than it looks — if it's clean it also replaces the
generated ambient bed in the reel.

## File 4 — your voice

1. Sit in the car, doors and windows shut. It's the quietest small room you own.
2. Open Voice Memos (iPhone) or the recorder app (Android).
3. Hold the phone a hand's width from your mouth, slightly off to one side so
   your breath doesn't thump the mic.
4. Say it at a normal, unhurried pace:

   > "I'm building Tuveloz because good mechanics shouldn't have to pay just
   > to quote a job. Provider signup is open."
   >
   > *(pause — a full second, count one thousand one)*
   >
   > "Customer jobs aren't live yet."

5. Stop after the last word.
6. **Record it twice.** Nobody gets it first try and it costs nothing.

Don't rush the pause. That gap is what makes the last sentence read as honesty
instead of small print.

## Mistakes that ruin the take

| Mistake | What happens |
|---|---|
| Holding the phone yourself | Only one hand works; reads as a selfie |
| Talking during the hand takes | Voice is coming from file 4; live audio just adds wind |
| Miming the work | Instantly obvious, kills the whole point |
| Turning a fastener | Unsafe, and casts you as a mechanic |
| Filming sideways | Unusable for Reels and TikTok |
| A tool logo facing camera | Someone else's trademark in your ad |
| Adding music, a filter or captions in the phone app | I can't remove them; send it raw |
| Trimming or exporting before sending | Quality loss and lost audio |
| One take only | No fallback when something small goes wrong |

## What to send me

All four files, **untouched** — no trimming, no filters, no captions, no export
presets, nothing that re-compresses on the way out.

Save them anywhere on the machine and paste me the file paths, the same way you
sent the ad. I'll check voice clarity, levels, framing, visible branding, plate
and address, pick the strongest take, and cut it.

## For next time only

Once you've done this once, the alternative is recording the line live while
you work, using a $20–30 wired clip-on mic with a furry windscreen tucked
inside your collar. Slightly more natural, more that can go wrong. Not for
this shoot.
