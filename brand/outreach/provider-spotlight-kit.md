# Provider Spotlight Kit

The highest-yield content the account will ever run, and the cheapest: one
photo and three questions.

Every other post reaches whoever the algorithm decides to show it to. A
spotlight reaches the provider's own customers, their family, and their
neighbors — people who already live in Montgomery County and already trust
that person. They share it because it's about them. That is local reach you
cannot buy at any price, and it recruits the next provider better than any ad,
because a mechanic seeing a mechanic they recognize is proof the thing is real.

Run one per accepted provider, ongoing. See
`audience-growth-playbook.md` §3 for where this sits in the overall plan.

---

## Consent comes first — this is not optional

**The Provider Agreement contains no publicity or likeness clause.** Nothing in
it grants Tuveloz the right to use a provider's name, business name, photo, or
likeness in marketing. So permission has to be asked for and given explicitly,
every time, in writing.

Rules:

- Get a written "yes" before anything is posted. A DM reply saying yes is fine;
  a verbal yes on a phone call is not.
- **Save the reply.** Screenshot or export it to the provider's record.
- Name exactly what you'll use and where: their first name, business name,
  photo, town, and services, on Tuveloz's social accounts.
- Honor removal on request, no questions asked and no delay. Say so up front —
  it makes the yes easier to give.
- If a provider gives you a photo with another person in it (a customer, an
  employee, a family member), don't post it. You have that provider's consent,
  not theirs.
- Never post a provider who is still under review. Accepted only.

## The three questions

Short on purpose. A provider working out of a truck will answer three
questions and ignore seven.

1. **How long have you been doing this, and how'd you get into it?**
   → the human hook, and the line people actually remember.
2. **What's the job you get called for most?**
   → concrete, local, and doubles as free SEO-ish keyword content.
3. **What's the one thing you wish more car owners knew?**
   → this is the shareable line. It turns an ad into something useful, which
   is the difference between a post people scroll past and one they send to a
   friend.

Optional fourth if they're chatty: *What made you sign up for something that
hasn't launched yet?* — the answer is usually the best provider-recruitment
copy you'll ever write, because it's in their words, not yours.

## The photo

Ask for **one**, and make it easy:

- Them with their vehicle or their tools, outdoors, daylight. A phone photo is
  fine — polished looks fake, real looks real.
- Vertical if they have it (fits Reels/Stories); square works.
- No customers, no kids, no readable plates, no house numbers.
- Their business logo or truck lettering visible is a bonus — it's their brand
  getting airtime, which is part of what they're saying yes for.

If they won't send a photo of themselves, that's common and fine. A clean shot
of the van, the toolbox, or hands mid-job works nearly as well, and some people
will only say yes to that version.

## Post template

**On-image text (top third, keep it short):**

```
Meet [First name]
[Service] · [Town], MD
```

**Caption (EN):**

```
Meet [First name] — [service] out of [Town]. 🔧

[Answer to Q1, tightened to one or two sentences.]

Gets called most for: [answer to Q2].

What [he/she/they] wish more car owners knew: "[answer to Q3]"

[First name] is one of the first providers signed up on Tuveloz for
Montgomery County. Customer requests aren't open yet — when they are,
[he/she/they]'ll be there on day one.

Independent providers: joining is free and you keep 100% of what you quote →
tuveloz.com/join

#MontgomeryCountyMD #MoCo #[TownMD] #DMV #MobileMechanic #SupportLocal
#SmallBusiness #ShopLocal #Maryland
```

Use the pronouns the provider uses for themselves — ask if you don't know, or
write the sentence around it ("one of the first providers signed up…").

**Caption (ES)** — post the Spanish version to the same platforms when the
provider is Spanish-speaking. Their network is who this reaches, so it should
be in the language their network reads.

```
Le presentamos a [Nombre] — [servicio] en [ciudad]. 🔧

[Respuesta 1, en una o dos frases.]

Lo que más le piden: [respuesta 2].

Lo que quisiera que más conductores supieran: "[respuesta 3]"

[Nombre] es uno de los primeros proveedores inscritos en Tuveloz para el
Condado de Montgomery. Las solicitudes de clientes todavía no están abiertas —
cuando lo estén, estará listo desde el primer día.

Proveedores independientes: inscribirse es gratis y usted se queda con el 100%
de lo que cotiza → tuveloz.com/join
```

**Then do the one thing that makes it work:** send the provider the link and
ask them to share it. That single step is most of the reach. Tag their business
account if they have one so it lands in their followers' feeds too.

## Honesty rules (specific to spotlights)

Carried from `provider-outreach-kit.md`, plus two that only apply here:

- Never imply customers can book or pay today, or that this provider is
  currently taking Tuveloz jobs. They aren't — nobody is.
- Never imply Tuveloz vetted skill or endorses quality. Acceptance means the
  application and required evidence passed review. A spotlight is an
  introduction, not a recommendation, and the caption should never read like
  one.
- Don't state or imply credentials — licenses, certifications, insurance —
  unless that specific credential is verified in their record. "Been doing
  this 12 years" is their claim, in their voice, and is fine. "Licensed and
  insured" is a factual assertion Tuveloz would be making.
- No income claims, theirs or implied.

## Cadence

One spotlight per week once you have a few accepted providers — enough to be a
reliable slot in the calendar, not so much that the feed becomes only this.
Bank the answers as they come in; a provider who replies today doesn't have to
post today.

Ask every provider at acceptance, while the good feeling is fresh. The yes rate
right after "you're approved" is far higher than it is three weeks later.
